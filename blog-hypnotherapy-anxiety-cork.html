Hypnotherapy for Anxiety: A Practical Guide

John A O'Connor, Clinical Hypnotherapist 11 June 2026 9 minute read

Anxiety is the single most common reason people get in touch. It is also the one where the gap between knowing what is happening and being able to stop it is widest.

You may already understand your anxiety very well. You may be able to explain exactly why the fear is disproportionate, and still find your body responding as though it is not. That gap is the thing this article is about.

Why understanding it is not the same as resolving it

Anxiety is not primarily a thinking problem, which is why thinking your way out of it works so poorly. The response is generated by parts of the nervous system that do not consult your reasoning first.

This is why the advice to calm down, or the observation that there is nothing to worry about, lands so badly. It is addressed to the part of you that already agrees.

Hypnotherapy works at a different level. Rather than arguing with the anxious thought, it works with the state underneath it, aiming to change the automatic response rather than to reason with it after the fact.

What actually happens in the work

In a focused, relaxed state, attention narrows and the usual critical filtering softens. That is the state in which suggestion and imagery have more traction than they do in ordinary alert conversation.

Practically, the work tends to involve identifying the specific pattern rather than treating anxiety as one undifferentiated thing. Anticipatory anxiety before events, generalised background worry, social anxiety and panic all behave differently and are approached differently.

Most people also learn self-hypnosis or a comparable technique to use between sessions. That matters, because the goal is not dependence on appointments.

What the evidence supports, honestly

There is reasonable evidence for hypnotherapy in anxiety, including trials where it performs comparably to cognitive behavioural approaches, and meta-analyses showing benefit over no treatment. It is a legitimate option rather than a fringe one.

It is not, however, a replacement for medical care where that is needed. Anxiety that involves significant depression, self-harm, or that has a possible physical cause should be assessed by a GP first. A responsible hypnotherapist will say so rather than take the booking.

How many sessions it usually takes

Most clients complete a course of between three and eight sessions. Anxiety typically sits in that range rather than being resolved in one.

People often notice something shifting earlier than they expect, frequently around the third or fourth session, but the later sessions are what tend to make the change hold. Stopping at the first improvement is one of the more common reasons results do not last.

Anxiety that shows up in the body

A large proportion of anxiety presents physically first: the tight chest, the disrupted sleep, the gut that reacts before you have consciously registered anything is wrong.

This is not imagination. The gut-brain axis is a measurable physical pathway, which is why gut-directed hypnotherapy has a genuine evidence base for IBS. Where anxiety and physical symptoms are tangled together, they are usually worked on together rather than separately.

If you have already tried other things

Most people who arrive have. Books, apps, breathing exercises, sometimes counselling or medication. Those are not failures and they are not wasted.

What tends to bring people to hypnotherapy is wanting to work below the level of deliberate management. Not better strategies for coping with the response, but a change in the response itself. Whether that is the right description of what you want is a reasonable thing to establish in a first conversation.

If anxiety is currently severe, or if there is any risk to your safety, please speak to your GP or contact the Samaritans on 116 123. Hypnotherapy is not an emergency service and should not be your first port of call in a crisis.

Frequently asked questions

Does hypnotherapy work for anxiety?
There is reasonable evidence supporting it, including trials showing it performs comparably to cognitive behavioural approaches. Results vary between individuals, and it works best where you are motivated and open to the process.
How many sessions will I need for anxiety?
Typically somewhere between three and eight. You will be given a realistic estimate after the first conversation rather than a fixed number up front.
Can I have hypnotherapy while taking medication for anxiety?
Yes, and many people do. Hypnotherapy does not interact with medication. Do not change or stop any prescription without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.
Will I lose control during a session?
No. You remain aware throughout and cannot be made to do or say anything against your will. The state is closer to being absorbed in a film than to being unconscious.
Is online as effective for anxiety work?
The research comparing online and in-person delivery finds broadly comparable outcomes. For people who find leaving the house itself difficult, starting online removes a real barrier.
