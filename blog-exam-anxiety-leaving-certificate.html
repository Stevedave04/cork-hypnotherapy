Exam Anxiety and the Leaving Certificate

John A O'Connor, Clinical Hypnotherapist 25 August 2026 8 minute read

There is a particular kind of frustration in watching a student who knows the material walk out of an exam having produced almost none of it. The preparation was done. The knowledge is there. Something else got in the way.

That gap between what a student knows and what they can access under pressure is what exam anxiety actually is, and it is one of the more treatable things hypnotherapy works with.

Why knowing the material is not enough

Under significant stress the body prioritises immediate threat response over complex thought. That is useful if something is about to hit you and unhelpful in a school hall in June.

The practical effect is that working memory narrows and recall becomes harder. The student is not being lazy or underprepared. They are experiencing a physiological response that is actively interfering with the function they need most.

This is why telling a student to relax achieves so little. They already know they should be calm. The response is not being generated by the part of them that is listening.

The Leaving Cert makes it worse in specific ways

The Irish system concentrates enormous weight into a short window. A student's options can appear to hinge on a fortnight, which is a lot of pressure to sit on an eighteen year old.

The points system makes the stakes feel numerically precise, and the timetable means several high-stakes exams land within days of each other. There is very little recovery room if one goes badly, so a poor paper on day one can contaminate the rest of the fortnight.

Anticipatory anxiety in the months beforehand is often as damaging as anxiety on the day, because it degrades the study that the whole thing depends on.

What hypnotherapy actually works on

The aim is not to make the student care less. It is to make the knowledge accessible under pressure.

Work typically covers the physical stress response, so that the body does not escalate at the start of a paper; recall under pressure, so that revision translates into performance; and confidence, which for many students has been eroded by a run of mock results.

Students usually also learn a technique they can use themselves, in the hall, without anyone noticing. That matters, because the exam is the one place a therapist cannot be.

Timing: start earlier than feels necessary

The most common mistake is leaving it until May. By then the student is exhausted, the anxiety is entrenched, and there is no time for anything to consolidate.

Starting in autumn or early in the new year means the work supports the study itself, not just the fortnight of exams. It also means mocks become useful practice rather than a source of fresh evidence that things are going badly.

Exampass is a dedicated programme for exactly this, with full detail at exampass.ie.

What parents can usefully do

Some honesty here: parental anxiety transmits, and reassurance delivered anxiously does not reassure.

The most useful things are unglamorous. Protect sleep, because sleep deprivation degrades recall more than an extra hour of revision improves it. Keep the conversation about effort rather than predicted points. Avoid comparisons with siblings or classmates entirely.

And if a student is genuinely struggling rather than simply nervous, involve the school and the GP. Exam stress and a mental health difficulty are not the same thing, and the second needs proper support.

Not just the Leaving Certificate

The same work applies to Junior Cycle, university exams, repeat students, professional examinations, and driving tests. Anywhere performance under observation collapses despite adequate preparation, the mechanism is broadly the same.

If a student is showing signs of sustained low mood, withdrawal, or distress beyond exam nerves, speak to the school and your GP. Exam anxiety and depression can look similar from outside and need different help.

Frequently asked questions

How many sessions does a student usually need?
Commonly a short course rather than a single appointment, with the exact number depending on how entrenched the anxiety is and how much time remains before the exams.
When should we start?
Earlier than most people do. Autumn or early in the new year lets the work support revision itself rather than just the exam fortnight.
Will it make my child complacent?
No. The aim is to remove the interference between knowledge and recall, not to reduce motivation. Students generally study more effectively once the anxiety drops, because avoidance falls away.
Can sessions be done online around school?
Yes, and for students this is often easier to fit around school, study and travel.
Does this work for repeat students?
Often particularly well, because a repeat student is usually carrying a specific bad memory of the previous attempt, which is exactly the kind of thing this work addresses.
