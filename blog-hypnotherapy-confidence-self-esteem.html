Hypnotherapy for Confidence and Self-Esteem

John A O'Connor, Clinical Hypnotherapist 3 August 2026 8 minute read

Confidence is the thing everyone tells you to have and nobody can tell you how to get. Believe in yourself is not advice, it is a description of the destination.

The reason it is hard to build deliberately is that it is not really a belief you hold consciously. It is closer to an assumption you operate from, and assumptions are not changed by being argued with.

Where low confidence usually comes from

It is generally learned, and often earlier than people expect. A teacher's comment, a period of being excluded, a parent who was hard to satisfy, a job that went badly at a formative moment.

What forms is a working assumption about yourself that then filters everything afterwards. Evidence that contradicts it gets discounted as luck or as having fooled people. Evidence that supports it gets weighted heavily. That is why external success so often fails to shift it.

Which is also why people who are objectively very capable can be entirely sincere in feeling that they are not.

Why encouragement does not work

Being told you are good at something by someone who likes you is easy to discount, and most people with low confidence have become expert at discounting it.

The assumption sits below the level where that argument takes place. This is the same reason that logic does not resolve a phobia: you are addressing the wrong part of the system.

What the work actually involves

Two things, roughly. Reducing the automatic anxiety response in the situations where confidence collapses, and working on the underlying assumption itself.

In practice that often means identifying where it came from, not to dwell on it but because an assumption formed at fourteen is easier to loosen once it is recognised as an old conclusion rather than a fact.

It also usually means rehearsing the specific situations under hypnosis: the meeting, the presentation, the social event, so the nervous system has a version of it that did not go badly.

Confidence at work

This is the most common presentation. Presenting, speaking up in meetings, being newly promoted into a role that feels oversized, or dealing with a colleague who reliably makes you feel small.

Public speaking is worth naming separately because it is so widespread and responds well. Most people are not afraid of speaking, they are afraid of being visibly anxious while speaking, which is a self-feeding problem: worrying about showing nerves generates the nerves that then show.

Team and workplace sessions are available where this affects a group rather than an individual.

Confidence socially and in performance

Socially, low confidence often shows up as avoidance that has been rationalised. Declining invitations, keeping conversations short, not putting yourself in the way of new people. The avoidance keeps the assumption intact by removing any chance of contradicting it.

In sport, the same mechanism appears as a player who does not want the ball at a critical moment. That is confidence, not skill, and it is worked with directly in the sports side of the practice.

What a realistic outcome looks like

Not becoming a different person. Most people do not want that, and promising it would be daft.

A more realistic description is that the running commentary quietens, the situations that used to require bracing stop requiring it, and the gap narrows between what you are capable of and what you actually attempt.

If low self-esteem comes with persistent low mood, hopelessness, or thoughts of self-harm, please speak to your GP. That is depression rather than confidence, and it needs proper support. The Samaritans are on 116 123.

Frequently asked questions

How long does confidence work take?
Usually a course rather than a single session, commonly in the three to eight range, because an assumption held for years does not typically shift in one appointment.
Will I have to talk about my childhood?
Only where it is relevant and only as far as you want to. The focus stays on the present pattern; origins come up when they explain something useful.
Can this help with public speaking specifically?
Yes, and it is one of the more common requests. The fear is usually of visibly showing nerves rather than of speaking itself, which is a workable target.
Is this the same as assertiveness training?
No. Assertiveness training teaches behaviours to apply consciously. This works on the underlying assumption that makes those behaviours feel unavailable in the first place. They complement each other.
Do you work with businesses on this?
Yes, through team workshops and confidential one-to-one sessions delivered on site or online.
