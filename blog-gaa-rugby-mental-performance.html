The Mental Side of GAA and Rugby

John A O'Connor, Clinical Hypnotherapist 24 August 2026 8 minute read

Every club has had the season where the football was there and the results were not. The training was done, the panel was strong, and the tight games kept going the other way.

That pattern is rarely about fitness or skill, because by championship both sides usually have plenty of each. It is about what happens to a player in the last ten minutes of a one-point game.

The gap between training form and match form

Almost every player has a version of the same experience: striking the ball perfectly in training all week, then not being able to find the same action on Sunday.

Under pressure, attention narrows and the body tightens. Skills that have become automatic revert to being consciously controlled, which is precisely what makes them worse. A free-taker who suddenly starts thinking about their technique is a free-taker who is about to miss.

This is not a character flaw and it is not about wanting it more. It is a predictable physiological response, and like anything predictable it can be trained.

What actually gets worked on

Composure in the closing stages, so that the last ten minutes are played rather than survived.

Refocusing after a mistake. This is the single most useful skill in field sports, because the cost of a dropped ball is rarely the ball itself, it is the next five minutes spent thinking about it.

A pre-match routine that puts a player in a consistent state rather than leaving it to how they happen to feel that day.

And for some players, a specific block: the free-taker who has lost the shot, the goalkeeper whose puck-outs have gone, the player coming back from a serious injury who is not fully committing to contact.

The injury comeback, which nobody talks about

A cruciate injury is not only a physical rehabilitation. Players return physically cleared and still find themselves protecting the knee, half a second late into contact, not trusting the leg.

That hesitancy is often what causes the second injury. Working on the fear directly is not soft, it is a practical part of returning to full performance.

How it works with a club

Squad work usually runs as group sessions with the panel, covering the shared skills: composure, refocusing, pre-match routine. That builds a common language the management can then use during the week.

One-to-one sessions are added where a specific player has a specific issue, and those stay confidential.

Sessions can be delivered at the club rather than requiring a panel to travel, which for most clubs is the only workable arrangement during a season.

What it will not do

It will not turn an average side into a good one. If the football is not there, the mental side will not manufacture it.

What it can do is close the gap between how a team trains and how it plays, which for a squad already close to the level is often where the season is decided. Anyone promising more than that is selling something.

Beyond GAA and rugby

The same work applies across soccer, tennis, golf, track and field, and Irish and ballroom dancing, all of which are worked with here. The sports differ; the mechanism under pressure does not.

The clubs that get most from this start in pre-season rather than mid-championship. Introducing new mental skills the week of a quarter-final is too late for them to become automatic.

Frequently asked questions

Is this sports psychology?
It overlaps but is not the same. Sports psychology is a distinct profession with its own training and registration. This is hypnotherapy and performance work applied to sport, and the difference is worth understanding before choosing.
Do you work with whole panels or individuals?
Both. Group sessions for the squad, with confidential one-to-one work for players who need something specific.
Can you come to the club?
Yes. Sessions are delivered at the club across West Cork, which is generally the only practical option during a season.
When in the season should we start?
Pre-season. The skills need repetition before they hold up under championship pressure.
Does it work for younger players?
Yes, with appropriate consent from a parent or guardian for anyone under 18, and with the content pitched to the age group.
