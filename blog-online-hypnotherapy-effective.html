Online Hypnotherapy: Is It As Effective As In-Person?

John A O'Connor, Clinical Hypnotherapist 6 July 2026 7 minute read

It is a fair question to ask before booking. Something about hypnotherapy feels like it ought to require physical presence, a quiet room, a particular atmosphere. If you are considering online sessions, you want to be sure you are not settling for a diluted version of the real thing.

The short answer is: for most people and most conditions, online clinical hypnotherapy is just as effective as in-person work. The longer answer explains why that is, what the research actually shows, and what practical conditions make the difference.

What the research shows

The growth of telehealth during and after 2020 generated a substantial body of research comparing remote and in-person therapy outcomes. The findings across multiple modalities, including cognitive behavioural therapy, counselling, and hypnotherapy, have been consistently encouraging for remote delivery.

A 2021 review published in the Journal of Medical Internet Research found that videoconference-delivered psychological therapies produced outcomes comparable to face-to-face therapy across anxiety, depression, and stress-related conditions. Hypnotherapy-specific studies have shown similar patterns, particularly for IBS, anxiety, and habit change work.

The reason is not surprising once you understand how hypnotherapy actually works. Hypnosis is a state of focused inward attention. It takes place entirely in the client's own mind. The therapist provides guidance, framing, and suggestion through voice alone. Physical proximity is not a meaningful variable in that process.

The therapeutic relationship, clear communication, and a quiet and comfortable environment matter enormously. Whether the therapist is in the same room or on a screen matters considerably less than most people expect.

Why the medium does not change the mechanism

When you enter hypnosis, your attention turns inward. You are not interacting with the room around you. You are not focused on where the therapist is sitting. You are following a voice, processing language, engaging with imagery and suggestion at a level beneath conscious thought.

This is why hypnotherapy can work through audio recordings, why self-hypnosis is a learnable and effective practice, and why a well-conducted video session produces results consistent with an in-clinic one. The voice carries everything the session requires.

What does matter is the quality of the connection between therapist and client. That relationship, built through conversation before and after the hypnotic work, is not diminished by a screen. In some respects, the familiarity of your own home can support rather than hinder the relaxation required.

In-person versus online: an honest comparison

Online sessions

  • No travel time or cost
  • Available anywhere in Ireland
  • Your own familiar environment
  • Easier to schedule around work
  • Same outcomes for most conditions
  • Ideal if leaving the house is itself a barrier

In-clinic sessions

  • Dedicated, purpose-prepared space
  • No technology to manage
  • May suit those who find screens distracting
  • Some clients prefer the ritual of travelling to a session
  • Available at Ardfield, West Cork

Neither format is categorically better. The right choice depends on your circumstances, your preferences, and what helps you feel settled before a session begins.

Who benefits most from online sessions

Online hypnotherapy tends to work particularly well for certain groups of people.

People outside West Cork. John A works with clients across Ireland by video. If you are in Dublin, Galway, Limerick, or anywhere in between, distance is not a barrier.

People with busy schedules. Removing a commute from a session that might otherwise take half a day out of a working week makes a meaningful practical difference. Many clients find that being able to transition back into their normal environment immediately after a session suits them well.

People whose presenting issue involves leaving the house. For those working on social anxiety, agoraphobia, or generalised anxiety that makes travel feel difficult, starting with online sessions removes an unnecessary first hurdle. You engage with the therapeutic work without having to manage an additional stressor to get there.

People who work or live abroad. A number of John A's clients are Irish people living in the UK or Europe. Online sessions make continuity of care straightforward.

What you need for a good online session

The practical requirements are simple but worth taking seriously. A session that is interrupted by noise, a poor connection, or an uncomfortable position will not serve you as well as one where those variables are managed.

  • A quiet room where you will not be disturbed for the duration of the session
  • A comfortable chair or somewhere you can sit or recline easily
  • A stable internet connection and a device with a working camera and microphone
  • Headphones or earphones, which improve audio quality and help focus attention
  • Letting people in your household know you are not to be interrupted
  • Turning off phone notifications for the hour

That is genuinely all that is required. Most clients find that after the first five minutes of a session, they have forgotten entirely that there is a screen involved.

What platform does John A use?

Online sessions are conducted via Zoom or a similar secure video platform. You will receive a link ahead of your appointment. No account or download is required on your end beyond what you already have.

The session structure is identical to an in-clinic appointment. There is a conversation before the hypnotic work to discuss what you want to achieve and how you are feeling that day. The hypnotherapy itself follows. There is time at the end to debrief and to answer any questions.

If you have never done hypnotherapy before, you may find it useful to read about what the research says about how hypnotherapy works before your first appointment, whether in person or online.

A note on technical difficulties

Occasionally a connection will drop or audio will cut out. This is a practical reality of video sessions and is handled simply: the call is rejoined or rescheduled if necessary. It does not affect the work in a meaningful way. In over a decade of practice, John A has found that clients adapt quickly and that the adjustment to the online format typically takes one session at most.
